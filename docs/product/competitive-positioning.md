# Competitive positioning

Research date: 2026-10-17. Sources are linked per reference. These are product observations, not
user research: **"What the user remembers" and "what people tell friends" are our inferences
from the core interaction. We did not interview users.** Claims that come only from a vendor's own
marketing, or that could not be confirmed, are marked *(unverified)*.

This is not a feature checklist. Each entry asks what the product **sells** and which single
action people carry away.

## What each reference sells

| Reference | Sells mainly | Memorable action | What people likely tell friends *(inference)* |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blinq | Convenience, professionalism | Tap card → browser profile | "I don't carry paper cards anymore" |
| Popl | Networking ROI (lead capture) | Scan a badge or tap → lead in CRM | "Our booth captured every lead" |
| HiHello | Personalisation, contact management | Share a designed card many ways | "My card has video and all my links" |
| AirDrop | Speed | Pick a face, it's there | "Just AirDrop it" (the verb is the product) |
| Quick Share + Tap to share | Speed, now with touch | Touch phones, accept | "Touch phones and it sends" |
| NameDrop | Delight, identity (Contact Poster) | Hold phones close, a poster appears | "Our phones swapped contacts with that animation" |
| LinkedIn QR / Find nearby | Networking (graph growth) | Show QR, they scan, invite | "Just add me on LinkedIn" |
| Bump (2009–2014) | Delight, speed | Knock phones together | "Bump phones" |
| Apple Wallet NFC passes | Status, convenience | Hold near reader at the gate | Nothing. It just works, and that is the point |
| Amiibo | Collectible identity, ritual | Put the figure on the pad, the game knows it | "My Link amiibo unlocked …" |
| Nothing | Self-expression, design identity | Glyph lights, transparent back | "Look at the back of my phone" |

Pattern: products people *name in conversation* sell **a gesture that has a result**: AirDrop,
Bump, NameDrop. Products that sell **features** (Popl, HiHello) are described by category instead
("a digital business card app"). Kipat's opportunity is the gesture plus a recognisable result.

## Blinq

Sources: [blinq.me digital business card](https://blinq.me/solutions/digital-business-card), [NFC cards](https://blinq.me/accessories/nfc-business-cards), [support: NFC accessories](https://support.blinq.me/en/articles/77774-setting-up-your-blinq-nfc-accessories), [support: saving on Android](https://support.blinq.me/en/articles/68045-saving-a-contact-to-an-android-device), [Dupple review, 2026](https://dupple.com/reviews/blinq).

- **CORE PROMISE** Never run out of business cards; share your details any way.
- **CORE INTERACTION** Tap an NFC card (sold separately) or scan a QR → a web profile opens in the browser → save contact.
- **WHAT USER REMEMBERS** "It opened a page with their details."
- **STRENGTH** Works for recipients with nothing installed. One live profile behind every method.
- **WEAKNESS** The recipient lands in a browser tab, a context switch. On Android, saving went through a .vcf download (per Blinq's own support article). Many send methods dilute the gesture.
- **WHAT KIPAT SHOULD LEARN** The non-installed recipient path is mandatory, not optional. Kipat's card currently has none (no web profile).
- **WHAT KIPAT MUST NOT COPY** A send menu with many equal methods; "lead" vocabulary.

## Popl

Sources: [App Store "Popl: AI Lead Capture"](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1503939262), [G2 listing](https://www.g2.com/products/popl-lead-capture-digital-business-cards), [support: getting started](https://support.popl.co/en/articles/11465818-getting-started-with-popl).

- **CORE PROMISE** Capture every lead at events and route it to your CRM.
- **CORE INTERACTION** Scan a badge, paper card or QR, or tap a Popl → enriched lead.
- **WHAT USER REMEMBERS** The sales team remembers the count of leads. The recipient remembers a form.
- **STRENGTH** Clear buyer (event marketing teams) with clear ROI. Works offline and syncs later.
- **WEAKNESS** The person becomes a lead. Lead-capture mode can show a form before the identity. "90% of Fortune 500" is the vendor's own claim *(unverified)*.
- **WHAT KIPAT SHOULD LEARN** Event booths are a real distribution channel, and offline capture matters.
- **WHAT KIPAT MUST NOT COPY** Leads, enrichment, a form before the person, CRM as the product.

## HiHello

Sources: [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1378114205), [features](https://www.hihello.com/features/digital-business-cards), [receiving a HiHello card](https://hihello.com/blog/receiving-a-hihello-card).

- **CORE PROMISE** A beautiful digital card plus a contact manager with notes, tags and "where you met".
- **CORE INTERACTION** Share a designed card by QR, link, text, email, Wallet, NFC, AirDrop. Scan paper cards with AI.
- **WHAT USER REMEMBERS** "Their card had a video."
- **STRENGTH** The contact manager records a timeline of when you met. That is the "context of meeting" Kipat also wants.
- **WEAKNESS** Too many channels and fields. Enterprise admin drift. The recipient is asked to join.
- **WHAT KIPAT SHOULD LEARN** Meeting context (when and where) is the part of contact management worth keeping.
- **WHAT KIPAT MUST NOT COPY** A page builder, video cards, tags-and-notes CRM, join prompts on the recipient's first view.

## AirDrop

Sources: [9to5Mac on the 10-minute limit](https://9to5mac.com/2022/12/07/airdrop-limit-everyone-ios-16/), [iMore](https://www.imore.com/iphone/airdrop-will-be-limited-to-just-10-minutes-in-some-cases-with-ios-162-installed), [XDA](https://www.xda-developers.com/hard-airdrop-strangers-next-year/).

- **CORE PROMISE** Send anything to someone nearby, instantly.
- **CORE INTERACTION** Share → pick a person or device → they accept.
- **WHAT USER REMEMBERS** The verb itself.
- **STRENGTH** Speed. The recipient is a *person* (a contact's face), not an IP or device id.
- **WEAKNESS** Open receiving was abused for unsolicited images. Apple now reverts "Everyone" to "Contacts Only" after 10 minutes, and its release notes cite unwanted requests.
- **WHAT KIPAT SHOULD LEARN** Nearby content transfer needs a consent model *before* abuse, and people should be shown as people.
- **WHAT KIPAT MUST NOT COPY** Open discoverability to strangers. Any "receive from everyone" mode.

## Quick Share and Android "Tap to share"

Sources: [Google Help: Tap to share](https://support.google.com/android/answer/17161144?hl=en), [fonearena, 2026-08-13](https://www.fonearena.com/blog/?p=489498), [android.com Quick Share](https://www.android.com/articles/quick-share-on-android), [Android Authority: AirDrop interop](https://www.androidauthority.com/quick-share-works-with-airdrop-3618023), [BetaNews, 2025-11-21](https://betanews.com/2025/11/21/google-updates-android-quick-share-for-compatibility-with-apple-airdrop/).

- **CORE PROMISE** Android's AirDrop. Since Android 17, touching two phones starts it.
- **CORE INTERACTION** Touch top edges → choose contact fields, or pick content from the Sharesheet → the other device accepts.
- **WHAT USER REMEMBERS** "Touch phones, it sends."
- **STRENGTH** Built into the OS, on by default on supported devices, no install. Bluetooth discovery plus a direct Wi-Fi link. AirDrop interoperability on some devices (Pixel 10 first).
- **WEAKNESS** Exchanges a *contact card* (fields), not a presented identity. Device support is limited: Pixel 6+ on Android 17, with Samsung foldables announced. Off in work profiles.
- **WHAT KIPAT SHOULD LEARN** **Phone-to-phone exchange and touch-to-send are becoming platform features.** Kipat cannot win on the gesture or on file transfer. It can win on what appears: a chosen identity, presented by its owner, that you decide to keep.
- **WHAT KIPAT MUST NOT COPY** Its role as a generic file pipe. Competing on transfer speed.

## NameDrop (interaction concept only)

Sources: [Apple Support: NameDrop](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/namedrop-iphone-share-contact-info-iph1b6c664b7/ios), [Apple: Secure NameDrop](https://support.apple.com/guide/personal-safety/secure-namedrop-ips97e16d3b1/web).

- **CORE PROMISE** Hold phones close and swap contact cards.
- **CORE INTERACTION** Proximity → animation → choose Share or Receive only → choose fields.
- **WHAT USER REMEMBERS** The animation and the Contact Poster: *a person's chosen look* appearing.
- **STRENGTH** It proves the emotional thesis: the moment of exchange can be delightful, and a self-designed poster makes it feel like identity, not data. Receive-only is a first-class option.
- **WEAKNESS** Apple-only. It creates a contact and nothing more: no relationship, no context, nothing to reopen later.
- **WHAT KIPAT SHOULD LEARN** The Reveal is Kipat's Contact Poster plus the moment, and "receive only" maps to "dismiss without Connect".
- **WHAT KIPAT MUST NOT COPY** Sparkle and glow animation (Kipat's motion rules forbid it).

## LinkedIn QR and Find nearby

Sources: [Global Dating Insights, Find nearby test](https://www.globaldatinginsights.com/news/linkedin-testing-new-find-nearby-feature/), [Career Sherpa](https://careersherpa.net/new-linkedin-features-you-should-know-about/). Neither is official LinkedIn help, and availability may have changed *(unverified)*.

- **CORE PROMISE** Grow your professional network from in-person meetings.
- **CORE INTERACTION** Show a QR or open Find nearby (Bluetooth, opt-in, visible only while on that page) → send an invitation.
- **WHAT USER REMEMBERS** "We connected on LinkedIn" (and then the feed).
- **STRENGTH** Everyone already has it. The graph is the value.
- **WEAKNESS** The in-person moment is a doorway to a feed. Find nearby shows strangers in a radius, which is a list, not a meeting.
- **WHAT KIPAT SHOULD LEARN** Find nearby's privacy rule: you are discoverable only while you are deliberately on that screen. Any future Kipat discovery should do the same.
- **WHAT KIPAT MUST NOT COPY** Feed, invitations, follower counts, discovery-by-radius.

## Bump (history)

Sources: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bump_(application)), [The Next Web, 2014](https://thenextweb.com/news/google-shutting-bump-flock-january-31).

- **CORE PROMISE** Bump phones to swap contacts and photos.
- **CORE INTERACTION** A physical knock, matched by server-side sensors and location, and both sides confirm.
- **WHAT USER REMEMBERS** The knock. Reported at 125 million downloads by Feb 2013.
- **STRENGTH** A physical verb people repeated in conversation.
- **WEAKNESS** A gesture without a lasting relationship or reason to return. Google acquired it in 2013 and shut it down in January 2014.
- **WHAT KIPAT SHOULD LEARN** **A delightful gesture is not a retention model.** Kipat needs Connections to give the tap an "after".
- **WHAT KIPAT MUST NOT COPY** Building the product around the transport.

## Apple Wallet NFC passes

Sources: [Passcreator on iOS 18 event tickets](https://www.passcreator.com/en/solutions/enhanced-event-tickets-in-apple-wallet), [Showpass NFC ticket guide](https://help.showpass.com/hc/en-us/articles/27552439294487-Ticket-Buyer-Guide-NFC-Tickets-Customer-Guide).

- **CORE PROMISE** Your ticket is already on your phone; hold it near the reader.
- **CORE INTERACTION** "Hold Near Reader" → gate opens.
- **WHAT USER REMEMBERS** Nothing, because the technology disappeared.
- **STRENGTH** NFC as an invisible, trusted ritual. Screenshots don't work, so the credential is real.
- **WEAKNESS** Not a social object.
- **WHAT KIPAT SHOULD LEARN** Say "hold near", never "NFC". Authenticity should come from cryptography (NTAG 424 DNA later), not styling.
- **WHAT KIPAT MUST NOT COPY** Wallet-pass visual language for the Reveal. A Kipat identity is not a ticket.

## Amiibo

Sources: [Nintendo IR Q&A](https://www.nintendo.co.jp/ir/en/events/141030qa/index.html), [NFCW](https://www.nfcw.com/?p=29632).

- **CORE PROMISE** Your figure comes alive in the game.
- **CORE INTERACTION** Place the figure on the pad → the game recognises *this* character. Some games write data back.
- **WHAT USER REMEMBERS** "My figure unlocked something."
- **STRENGTH** A tangible object that is *recognised*. The object carries identity, and the screen confirms it.
- **WEAKNESS** Collecting pressure and per-game fragmentation.
- **WHAT KIPAT SHOULD LEARN** The object is the identity and the screen is its voice. That is exactly card → Reveal.
- **WHAT KIPAT MUST NOT COPY** Collectible editions as a business model. Gamified unlocks.

## Nothing

Sources: [Design Milk](https://design-milk.com/nothing-says-something-about-the-monochromatic-landscape-of-smartphones/), [91mobiles review](https://www.91mobiles.com/hub/nothing-phone-3).

- **CORE PROMISE** A phone that expresses taste.
- **CORE INTERACTION** Glyph lights, a transparent back, consistent design language from box to wallpaper.
- **WHAT USER REMEMBERS** "Look at the back of my phone."
- **STRENGTH** One signature element, applied consistently, starts conversations.
- **WEAKNESS** Critics question whether the signature is useful (review opinions).
- **WHAT KIPAT SHOULD LEARN** One signature (the Reveal), applied consistently across every transport, builds a brand. Self-expression is a legitimate reason to buy an object.
- **WHAT KIPAT MUST NOT COPY** Lights and effects for their own sake.

## Where Kipat sits

```
                      PRESENTS AN IDENTITY
                               ▲
              NameDrop ●       │        ● Kipat (target)
                               │
  ONE-OFF ◄────────────────────┼────────────────────► KEEPS A RELATIONSHIP
  EXCHANGE                     │
   Quick Share/Tap to share ●  │   ● HiHello   ● LinkedIn
   AirDrop ●   Bump ●          │   ● Blinq  ● Popl (as leads)
                               ▼
                    MOVES DATA / FIELDS
```

Kipat's quadrant: **a presented identity (the Reveal), kept by choice (Connect, Connections),
delivered by a physical act (phone or card).** Nobody above combines all three. Platform features
own the bottom-left. Card vendors own the bottom-right, which is a commodity.
