# Card direction scorecard

Each criterion is scored 1–5, where 5 is best. The exception is **AI-slop risk**, where 1 means very low risk and 5 means very high. Any direction with a slop risk above 2 had to be redesigned before scoring. Scores were set after building every direction as vector artwork, reviewing the proofs, and checking them against the research in `../research/reference-matrix.md` and the 12 review questions in the `kipat-card-design` skill. They are design judgement, not user data.

## Redesigns forced by the gate (risk above 2 before revision)

| Direction | First draft | Why it failed | Revision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aperture | Hexagonal opening, 32° twist | Read as a literal camera shutter or photo-app icon (risk 3) | Round opening, 24° slanted cuts, flat segments, no chrome |
| Frosted Contact | Flat grey proof, invisible rings, white underbase rendered with a stroke halo | Read as a cheap grey card, with a halo that looked AI-made around the text (risk 3) | White-ink rings shown in the proof, underbase follows the glyphs exactly, translucency honestly simulated and labelled |
| Layered Depth | Rounded corner on the contact point | The point floated off the plane and read as a misprint (risk 3) | Square corner exactly on the point, plane lifted one tonal step |

## Finalists for visual development

| Role | Direction | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | **Graphite Precision** | Highest desirability with top professional appeal. It is the clearest physical echo of Kipat's contact motif (the app mark *is* a dot with orbit arcs). It uses proven PVC finishes: soft-touch plus raised gloss, as on Dot. Dark, but not "black card with blue neon": the orbit is tonal and nothing glows. |
| **Secondary** | **Architectural Line** | Highest subtotal. It is the strongest light design, the cheapest and most reliable to print, and works equally for a student, an organisation or a founder. It proves Kipat isn't a black-card brand. |
| **Experimental** | **Frosted Contact** | Most distinctive and most "ano 'yan?". It is material-led and the best student appeal, but depends on an unproven stock sample (frost, white ink, antenna visibility). It needs a photo study to judge, which is exactly what the experimental slot is for. |

Not selected for photography, and why:
- **Aperture** scores well and stays a strong future edition, but its camera association needs real-world testing first.
- **Essential Mono** is the baseline: it needs no visual development, only a print proof.
- **Layered Depth** is the weakest flat proof and the highest production risk (deboss).
