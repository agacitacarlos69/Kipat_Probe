# Physical card reference matrix

Research done in October 2026 against live sources. It covers 20 shipped products and systems in four groups: premium financial cards, premium print, NFC identity competitors, and industrial and editorial identity.

Some vendor sites refused automated fetches: Revolut, Popl's shop, Blinq's shop and parts of Apple's coverage. For those, the details come from the vendor's own search snippets, press coverage, retailer listings or competitor reviews. "Unverified" means the detail could not be confirmed from a reachable source. Payment-card typefaces are almost never documented, so they are listed as unverified rather than guessed.

Kipat studies these for **restraint, proportion, material, finish and recognisability**. Kipat is not a bank, so payment-card trade dress is out of bounds:
- a chip-like rectangle on the left
- holograms
- embossed digits
- network-style corner marks
- tier words such as "Metal" or "Ultra"

## What the matrix decides

1. **Subtraction plus one owned signal** (A1, A5, D4). Each front has a small Kipat lockup, the edition's one motif, and the contact point.
2. **The contact point is functional** (D1, C5): it marks where to tap. Cobalt is used only there (D2). It is never red and never a logo (D4).
3. **Material honesty** (A4, B5): excellent matte, soft-touch or frosted PVC. No faux metal, no "laced" brushed print, no metallic ink.
4. **Recognition by touch** (B3, C6): soft-touch base plus one raised clear gloss, which is non-conductive and safe over the antenna.
5. **A cobalt core at the edge is a candidate signature** (B1, A3). It is a vendor question, not assumed.
6. **No metal or foil over the coil** (C2, C4, C5, NFC facts). If metal ever ships, it is a hybrid with a PVC tap face.
7. **QR on the back, plain and square** (C1, C3, C4): graphite on light, 4-module quiet zone, never stylised.
8. **The card belongs to the person** (C1, C2, C6): Kipat is small; identity lives on the back, with an optional monogram on the front.
9. **No bank trade dress**: no chip rectangle, hologram, embossed digits, network corner or tier word.

## NFC and print facts used

- **Metal detunes NFC** (eddy currents, inductive loading): printplast.com/how-metal-rfid-cards-work/ · iotforall.com/nfc-metallic-interference-ferrite-solutions.
  - Fixes are a ferrite layer, a slot or window, or a non-metal tap face.
- **Foil or metallic ink over the antenna: no vendor clearance found.** A patent describes metallic inks and foils acting as RF shielding (US 10599968). Treat it as a risk until a printed sample passes tap tests.
- **CR80 print**: bleed 2–3.2 mm depending on vendor (Prontaprint 3 mm, Plastic Printers ⅛ in, Worldline 2 mm). Safe zone 2–3 mm. Corner radius about 3 mm.
- **Minimum type**: ≥ 6 pt, reversed ≥ 8 pt (PLI, snippet). Finish text ≥ 10 pt, finish lines ≥ 1 pt. Registration about ±0.5 mm.
- **QR**: ≥ 4-module quiet zone (ISO/IEC 18004). Dark on light. Do not stylise.
- **Chips**: NTAG213/215/216 hold 144/504/888 bytes (NXP datasheet); a linked Kipat card needs 69. NTAG 424 DNA adds SUN (AES-128) for authenticity.
- **Antenna**: placement depends on the inlay; examples are 30 × 60 mm (GoToTags) and 70 × 40 mm (vendor datasheet, unverified). Get coil coordinates from the manufacturer.
